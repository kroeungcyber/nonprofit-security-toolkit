# 🤖 AI Tool Usage Policy

**A simple, free policy for safely using ChatGPT, Copilot, and other AI tools with your organization's data.**

---

## Why This Matters

**The Problem:** Volunteers and staff are already using AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot to save time — drafting grant letters, summarizing meeting notes, answering donor emails. That's often fine. The risk is what gets pasted in along the way.

**What can go wrong:**
- A volunteer pastes a donor list into ChatGPT to "clean up the formatting" — donor names, emails, and giving history now sit on a third-party server outside your control
- Someone asks an AI tool to draft a letter using a beneficiary's case notes, exposing sensitive personal or health information
- Free-tier consumer AI tools may use submitted data to train their models by default — meaning your data could resurface in another user's answers
- Board minutes, financial data, or passwords get pasted into a chat window "just to summarize"

**None of this requires malice.** It happens because AI tools are fast, free, and don't ask what kind of data you're giving them.

**The Solution:** A short, plain-English policy that tells volunteers exactly what's safe to put into an AI tool and what isn't — no technical background required.

---

## What You'll Accomplish

- ✅ A clear, one-page policy volunteers can actually follow
- ✅ Protection for donor, beneficiary, and financial data from accidental exposure
- ✅ Guidance on which AI tools are lower-risk for organizational use
- ✅ A plan for what to do if sensitive data was already shared with an AI tool

**Time:** 30-45 minutes to adopt and share
**Cost:** $0

---

## Step 1: Understand the Two Categories of AI Tools (10 minutes)

### Consumer / Free-Tier AI Tools
Examples: free ChatGPT, free Gemini, free Copilot (personal account)

**Risk:** Many free consumer tools use what you type to train their models by default, and don't offer a Data Processing Agreement. Treat anything typed here as **potentially public**.

### Business / Paid AI Tools with Data Protections
Examples: ChatGPT Team/Enterprise, Microsoft 365 Copilot (org account), Gemini for Workspace

**Lower risk, if:**
- Your organization has a business/enterprise account (not a personal one)
- The vendor offers a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) and states data isn't used for training
- IT/leadership has reviewed and approved the tool (see [Vendor Security Assessment](../05-keep-good-records/vendor-security-assessment.md))

**Rule of thumb:** If nobody at your organization approved the AI tool and read its data policy, treat it as consumer-grade and follow the "never paste" list below.

---

## Step 2: Adopt the "Never Paste This" List (10 minutes)

Share this list with everyone who uses AI tools for work:

**🚫 Never paste into any AI tool (unless it's an approved, org-managed business account):**
- Donor names, emails, addresses, or giving history
- Beneficiary/client case notes, especially health, immigration, or legal details
- Passwords, API keys, or account credentials
- Financial account numbers, tax IDs (EIN), or banking details
- Board meeting minutes or anything marked confidential
- Volunteer background check results or personal identification numbers

**✅ Generally fine to use AI tools for:**
- Drafting a generic email template (no real names/data filled in yet)
- Brainstorming program ideas or event themes
- Improving grammar/tone of already-public content (website copy, newsletters)
- Summarizing publicly available information (news articles, public reports)
- Writing code or scripts that don't contain real data or secrets

**When in doubt:** If you wouldn't post it on your public website, don't paste it into a free AI tool.

---

## Step 3: Set Ground Rules for Approved Business Tools (10 minutes)

If your organization has a paid/business AI account, still apply these rules:

1. **Use org accounts only** — never a personal ChatGPT/Gemini login for org work
2. **Still avoid pasting full donor databases or sensitive case files** — summarize or use placeholder data where possible
3. **Check the vendor's data policy** — confirm data isn't used for model training and can be deleted on request (this belongs in your [Vendor Security Assessment](../05-keep-good-records/vendor-security-assessment.md))
4. **Turn off "chat history" / "improve the model" settings** where the tool allows it

---

## Step 4: Share the Policy (10 minutes)

Send this short message to staff and volunteers:

```
Subject: Quick Policy — Using AI Tools (ChatGPT, Copilot, etc.) Safely

Hi team,

AI tools are great time-savers, and you're welcome to use them for work —
with one rule: don't paste in donor, beneficiary, financial, or password
information unless you're using our approved organizational account.

Quick guide:
✅ OK: brainstorming, drafting generic templates, improving public content
🚫 Not OK: donor lists, case notes, passwords, financial details, board minutes

If you're not sure, ask [contact person] before pasting anything sensitive.

Thanks for helping keep donor and client data safe!
```

---

## Step 5: If Sensitive Data Was Already Shared (As Needed)

If you discover sensitive data was pasted into a consumer AI tool:

1. **Don't panic, but don't ignore it** — treat it like a minor data exposure
2. **Delete the chat/conversation** in the AI tool's history if possible (note: this may not remove it from training data already processed)
3. **Check the vendor's privacy policy** for a data deletion request process and submit one
4. **Notify your Incident Commander** — see the [Incident Response Plan](../05-keep-good-records/incident-response-plan.md)
5. **Assess exposure** — was it donor PII, beneficiary data, or credentials? This determines if broader notification is needed
6. **Update the policy/training** based on how it happened
