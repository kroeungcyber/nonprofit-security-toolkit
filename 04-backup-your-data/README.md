# 💾 Backup Your Data

**Protect your organization's files from ransomware, theft, and accidents.**

---

## Why This Matters

**Data loss happens:**
- Hard drives fail (it's not a matter of if, but when)
- Laptops get stolen
- Ransomware encrypts all your files
- Someone accidentally deletes important documents
- Natural disasters damage equipment

**Without backups:**
- Years of donor records could disappear
- Financial records could be lost
- Grant applications and reports could be gone
- Your organization could be crippled

**Real story:** A small food bank was hit with ransomware. All their files were encrypted, including donor lists, volunteer schedules, and grant documents. They had no backups. They had to rebuild everything from scratch, costing them 6 months of work and nearly causing them to lose a major grant.

## Quick Start: Minimum Viable Backup

If you only have 1 hour:

1. **Enable Google Drive/File Stream** (30 min)
   - Sync important folders to Google Drive
   - This gives you cloud backup automatically

2. **Set up automatic backup** (30 min)
   - Windows: Enable File History or Windows Backup
   - Mac: Enable Time Machine to an external drive

**This gives you 2 copies of your important data.**

---

## The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

**The gold standard for backups:**

| Number | What It Means | Example |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **3** | Keep **3 copies** of important data | Original + 2 backups |
| **2** | Use **2 different types** of storage | Cloud + external hard drive |
| **1** | Keep **1 copy off-site** | Cloud storage counts as off-site |

**Example setup:**
1. **Original** — Files on your computer
2. **Local backup** — External hard drive at the office
3. **Cloud backup** — Google Drive, OneDrive, or Dropbox

## What Should You Back Up?

**Critical (must back up):**
- Donor database
- Financial records (QuickBooks, spreadsheets)
- Grant applications and reports
- Volunteer and staff contact information
- Email archives
- Website files and databases
- Important documents (bylaws, 501(c)(3) paperwork)

**Important (should back up):**
- Marketing materials
- Photos and videos
- Meeting minutes and board documents
- Program files and curriculum

**Nice to have (back up if space allows):**
- General correspondence
- Drafts and working documents

## Backup Methods Compared

| Method | Cost | Effort | Security | Best For |
|--------|------|--------|----------|----------|
| **Google Drive/OneDrive** | Free tier available | Low (automatic) | Medium | Daily work files |
| **External hard drive** | $50-100 one-time | Medium (manual or scheduled) | High | Large files, full system backup |
| **Cloud backup service (Backblaze)** | $7/month/computer | Low (automatic) | High | Complete computer backup |
| **NAS/Network storage** | $200-500 one-time | High (setup required) | High | Organizations with IT volunteers |

**Our recommendation for non-profits:**
1. **Google Drive** or **OneDrive** — for active files (free tier usually sufficient)
2. **External hard drive** — for full backups (buy once, use for years)
