# 📋 Compliance Quick Guide

**Essential data protection and privacy compliance for non-profits.**

---

## Why This Matters

Non-profits handle sensitive personal data: donor information, volunteer records, beneficiary data, and financial details. Data protection laws apply to you whether you're a small local organization or a large international non-profit.

**Laws that may apply to your organization:**
- **GDPR** (General Data Protection Regulation) — if you have EU donors/volunteers
- **CCPA/CPRA** (California Consumer Privacy Act) — if you have California donors
- **State privacy laws** — Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, and more
- **IRS requirements** — for donor record keeping
- **State charitable solicitation laws**

**Penalties for non-compliance:**
- GDPR: Up to €20 million or 4% of annual revenue
- CCPA: Up to $7,500 per violation
- Loss of donor trust and reputation damage

---

## What You'll Accomplish

- ✅ Understand which privacy laws apply to you
- ✅ Create required privacy notices
- ✅ Implement data retention policies
- ✅ Handle data subject rights requests
- ✅ Prepare for data breach notifications
- ✅ Document your compliance efforts

**Time to implement:** 4-6 hours initially, then 30 minutes/month maintenance  
**Cost:** $0 (templates and guidance provided)

## Step 1: Determine Which Laws Apply

### GDPR (European Union)

**Applies to you if:**
- You have donors, volunteers, or beneficiaries in the EU/EEA
- You process data of EU residents (even if you're US-based)
- You offer goods/services to EU residents

**Key requirements:**
- Privacy notice explaining data use
- Lawful basis for processing data
- Data retention limits
- Data subject rights (access, deletion, portability)
- 72-hour breach notification
- Data Processing Agreements with vendors

**Good news for non-profits:**
- Charitable purposes may qualify for some exemptions
- Reduced requirements if processing is "occasional"
- But donor/volunteer management is typically "regular" processing

---

### CCPA/CPRA (California)

**Applies to you if:**
- You have donors or volunteers in California
- You meet any of these thresholds:
  - Annual gross revenue > $25 million
  - Buy/sell/share personal information of 100,000+ CA residents
  - Derive 50%+ revenue from selling CA resident data

**Key requirements:**
- Privacy policy with specific disclosures
- "Do Not Sell My Personal Information" link (if applicable)
- Consumer rights (know, delete, opt-out, correct)
- 45-day response time for requests

**Most small non-profits:** Don't meet thresholds but should still have good privacy practices

---

### Other State Laws

**Virginia (VCDPA), Colorado (CPA), Connecticut (CTDPA), Utah (UCPA):**
- Similar to CCPA but different thresholds
- Generally apply to larger organizations
- Focus on consumer rights and transparency

**Recommendation:** Even if these don't legally apply, following their principles shows good stewardship of donor data.

## Step 2: Create Your Privacy Notice

**What it is:** A public document explaining how you collect, use, and protect personal data.

**Where to post it:**
- Your website footer
- Donation pages
- Volunteer application forms
- Email signatures

### Privacy Notice Template

```
PRIVACY POLICY
[Your Organization Name]
Last Updated: [Date]

1. WHO WE ARE
[Organization Name] is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization based in [City, State].
Contact: [Email] | [Phone] | [Address]

2. WHAT DATA WE COLLECT
We collect the following types of personal information:

From Donors:
- Name and contact information (email, phone, address)
- Payment information (processed by secure third parties)
- Donation history and preferences
- Tax ID numbers for major gifts

From Volunteers:
- Name and contact information
- Skills, availability, and interests
- Background check information (if applicable)
- Emergency contacts

From Beneficiaries/Program Participants:
- Name and contact information
- Demographic information (age, household size)
- Program participation records
- Case notes (kept confidential)

From Website Visitors:
- IP address and browser information
- Pages visited and time spent
- Cookies and tracking technologies

3. WHY WE COLLECT YOUR DATA (Lawful Basis)

Under GDPR, we process your data based on:
- Consent: When you sign up for newsletters or volunteer
- Contract: When you make a donation or register for programs
- Legal obligation: Tax reporting, charitable solicitation compliance
- Legitimate interests: Organizational communication, donor stewardship

4. HOW WE USE YOUR DATA
We use your personal information to:
- Process donations and send receipts
- Communicate about our programs and impact
- Recruit and manage volunteers
- Comply with legal and tax requirements
- Improve our services and website
- Prevent fraud and ensure security

We DO NOT:
- Sell your personal information to third parties
- Share your data for marketing purposes without consent
- Use your data for purposes unrelated to our mission

5. WHO WE SHARE DATA WITH
We only share your data with:
- Payment processors (Stripe, PayPal, etc.) — for donations only
- Email service providers (Mailchimp, etc.) — for communications you requested
- Cloud storage providers (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365)
- Legal authorities — when required by law
- Professional advisors (accountants, lawyers) — under confidentiality agreements

All third parties sign Data Processing Agreements requiring them to protect your data.

6. HOW LONG WE KEEP YOUR DATA

Donor Records:
- Contact information: 7 years after last donation (IRS requirement)
- Donation history: Permanently (for organizational records)
- Payment details: Immediately deleted after processing (we don't store credit cards)

Volunteer Records:
- Active volunteers: Duration of service + 3 years
- Background checks: 3 years after volunteer leaves

Program Participant Records:
- Service records: 7 years (funder/grant requirements)
- Case notes: Per program requirements, typically 3-7 years

Website Data:
- Analytics: 26 months (Google Analytics default)
- Cookies: Per browser settings, up to 13 months

7. YOUR RIGHTS

Depending on your location, you have the right to:

Right to Access: Request a copy of your personal data
Right to Correction: Update inaccurate information
Right to Deletion: Request deletion of your data (with some exceptions)
Right to Restrict Processing: Limit how we use your data
Right to Data Portability: Receive your data in a portable format
Right to Object: Opt out of certain processing (like marketing)
Right to Withdraw Consent: Unsubscribe from communications anytime

To exercise these rights, contact: [privacy@yourorg.org]
We respond within 45 days (CCPA) or 30 days (GDPR).

8. DATA SECURITY
We protect your data using:
- Encryption in transit (HTTPS/TLS)
- Access controls and authentication
- Regular security training for staff
- Data backup and recovery procedures
- Incident response plan

9. COOKIES AND TRACKING
We use:
- Essential cookies: Required for website functionality
- Analytics cookies: Understand how visitors use our site
- Marketing cookies: Only with your consent

You can manage cookie preferences through your browser settings.

10. CHILDREN'S PRIVACY
We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13 without parental consent.
If you believe we have inadvertently collected such data, contact us immediately.

11. INTERNATIONAL DATA TRANSFERS
If you're in the EU/EEA, your data may be transferred to the US. We ensure appropriate safeguards:
- Standard Contractual Clauses with vendors
- Data Processing Agreements
- Adequate security measures

12. CHANGES TO THIS POLICY
We may update this policy periodically. Check the "Last Updated" date.
Significant changes will be notified via email.

13. CONTACT US
For privacy questions or to exercise your rights:
[Organization Name]
[Address]
[Email]
[Phone]

If you're in the EU, you also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.
```

## Step 3: Implement Data Retention Policy

**Why this matters:** Keeping data longer than necessary increases risk and violates privacy laws.

### Data Retention Schedule Template

```

[Your Organization Name]

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Donation receipts           7 years (IRS)            Secure shred
Donor contact info          7 years after last gift  Digital deletion
Major donor tax letters     7 years                  Secure shred
Donor credit card data      Delete immediately       Secure deletion
Recurring donor agreements  Duration + 3 years       Digital deletion

VOLUNTEER RECORDS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Volunteer applications      3 years after departure  Digital deletion
Background checks           3 years after departure  Secure shred
Volunteer hours             3 years                  Digital deletion
Emergency contacts          Duration of service      Digital deletion

PROGRAM DATA
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Beneficiary contact info    Per program policy       Digital deletion
Case notes                  7 years (funder req)     Digital deletion
Program attendance          3 years                  Digital deletion
Evaluation surveys          3 years                  Digital deletion

FINANCIAL RECORDS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Bank statements             7 years (IRS)            Secure shred
Payroll records             7 years                  Secure shred
Grant applications          7 years                  Digital deletion
Audit reports               Permanently              Digital archive

COMMUNICATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Email (general)             3 years                  Digital deletion
Email (legal/contract)      7 years                  Digital archive
Newsletter subscriptions    Until unsubscribed       Digital deletion
Social media posts          Platform dependent       Platform deletion

WEBSITE/TECHNICAL
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Server logs                 90 days                  Automatic deletion
Analytics data              26 months                Automatic deletion
User account data           Until deletion requested Digital deletion
Backup files                90 days                  Secure deletion

SECURITY INCIDENTS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Incident reports            7 years                  Digital archive
Breach notifications        7 years                  Digital archive
Investigation records       7 years                  Secure shred

EXCEPTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Legal hold: If litigation is anticipated or pending, do not delete
relevant records regardless of retention schedule.

Archival: Records of historical significance may be retained
indefinitely in anonymized form with board approval.
```

### Annual Data Cleanup Procedure

**Schedule:** Conduct quarterly review, annual purge

**Process:**
1. Run reports of data older than retention periods
2. Verify no legal holds apply
3. Export important records to archive (if required)
4. Permanently delete from active systems
5. Document what was deleted and when
6. Update asset inventory

**Secure Disposal Methods:**
- **Paper:** Cross-cut shredding (not strip-cut)
- **Digital:** Use secure deletion tools (not just delete key)
- **Hard drives:** Physical destruction or certified wiping
- **Cloud:** Use provider's permanent deletion, not just trash

## Step 4: Handle Data Subject Rights Requests

**What are these:** People asking to see, correct, or delete their data.

### Request Tracking Log

```
DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS REQUEST LOG

Date Received | Requester Name | Type of Request | Status | Completed Date | Notes
──────────────┼────────────────┼─────────────────┼────────┼────────────────┼────────
              │                │                 │        │                │
              │                │                 │        │                │
              │                │                 │        │                │

Request Types:
- Access: Request copy of their data
- Correction: Update inaccurate information
- Deletion: Request data be deleted
- Restriction: Limit how data is used
- Portability: Receive data in usable format
- Objection: Object to processing
```

### Response Procedures

**Step 1: Verify Identity (Within 2 Days)**
- Request ID or other verification
- Confirm email address or phone number on file
- Document verification method

**Step 2: Assess Request (Within 5 Days)**
- Determine what data is involved
- Check for legal obligations that prevent deletion
- Identify systems where data is stored
- Estimate time required

**Step 3: Process Request (Within 30 Days GDPR / 45 Days CCPA)**

**For Access Requests:**
1. Export data from all relevant systems
2. Remove other people's personal data (redact)
3. Format in readable manner (PDF, spreadsheet)
4. Send securely (encrypted email or secure portal)

**For Deletion Requests:**
1. Identify all locations of the data
2. Check for legal holds or retention requirements
3. Delete from primary systems
4. Delete from backups (if technically feasible)
5. Notify third parties who received the data
6. Document deletion

**Exceptions to Deletion:**
- Legal obligation (tax records, contracts)
- Public interest (archiving, research)
- Legal claims (pending litigation)
- Vital interests (emergency contact info)

**Step 4: Respond**
- Provide data or confirm deletion
- Explain any delays or exceptions
- Offer appeal process if request denied
- Document completion

### Template Responses

**Access Request Response:**
```
Subject: Your Data Access Request - [Organization Name]

Dear [Name],

Thank you for your data access request received on [Date].

We have processed your request and attached a copy of your personal data.

What we found:
- Contact information: Name, email, phone, address
- Donation history: [Summary]
- Volunteer records: [Summary if applicable]
- Communication preferences: [Summary]

If you believe any information is inaccurate, please let us know.

If you have questions about this data, contact us at [email].

Best regards,
[Name]
[Organization]
```

**Deletion Confirmation:**
```
Subject: Your Data Deletion Request - Confirmation

Dear [Name],

We have completed your request to delete your personal data.

Deleted from:
- Donor database
- Email marketing system
- Volunteer management system
- Cloud storage

Data we must retain (legal obligation):
- Donation records for tax purposes (retained as required by IRS)
- These records contain minimal information and are secured separately

Your data has been permanently removed from all active systems.

Thank you for your support of our mission.

[Organization]
```

**Partial Deletion (With Explanation):**
```
Subject: Your Data Deletion Request - Partial Completion

Dear [Name],

We have processed your data deletion request. However, we must retain certain records due to legal obligations.

Deleted:
- Marketing contact lists
- Volunteer application (inactive)
- Email correspondence
- Program participation records (beyond retention period)

Retained (required by law):
- Donation receipts (IRS requirement, 7 years)
- This includes: name, donation amount, date, receipt number
- Access to these records is restricted to finance staff only

We have anonymized identifying information where possible.

If you have questions about this exception, please contact us.

[Organization]
```

## Step 6: Data Processing Agreements (DPAs)

**What these are:** Contracts with vendors who process your data, requiring them to protect it.

### When You Need a DPA

**Required for:**
- Cloud storage (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Dropbox)
- Email marketing (Mailchimp, Constant Contact)
- Donation processing (Network for Good, Classy)
- Database/CRM providers (Salesforce, Bloomerang)
- Accounting software (QuickBooks Online)
- Website hosting
- Payment processors

**Not typically required for:**
- Utilities (electric, internet)
- General office suppliers
- One-time consultants (if no ongoing data access)

### DPA Checklist

Your Data Processing Agreements should include:

✅ **Processor Obligations:**
- Process data only on your instructions
- Ensure confidentiality of staff
- Implement appropriate security measures
- Not engage sub-processors without approval
- Assist with data subject rights requests
- Assist with security breach notifications
- Delete or return data at end of contract
- Allow audits and inspections

✅ **Your Obligations:**
- Provide lawful instructions
- Ensure data subjects are informed
- Pay for services

✅ **Security Requirements:**
- Encryption in transit and at rest
- Access controls and authentication
- Regular security testing
- Incident response procedures
- Business continuity planning

✅ **Breach Notification:**
- Notify you within 24-48 hours
- Provide details of breach
- Cooperate with investigation
- Assist with notifications

✅ **Sub-processors:**
- List of approved sub-processors
- Same level of protection required
- Notification of changes

### Template DPA Request Email

```
Subject: Data Processing Agreement Required - [Organization Name]

Dear [Vendor],

[Organization Name] is committed to protecting the personal data of our donors,
volunteers, and beneficiaries. As a vendor who processes personal data on our
behalf, we need to ensure appropriate safeguards are in place.

Please provide a signed Data Processing Agreement (DPA) that addresses:

1. Data protection and security measures
2. Confidentiality obligations
3. Sub-processor management
4. Breach notification procedures (within 48 hours)
5. Data subject rights assistance
6. Data return/deletion at contract end
7. Audit rights

We require this DPA to comply with GDPR, CCPA, and our internal data
protection policies.

Please send the signed DPA to [email] by [date].

If you have questions or need to discuss specific terms, please contact me.

Thank you for your partnership in protecting personal data.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Title]
[Organization Name]
[Contact Information]
```

## Step 7: International Data Transfers

**The Issue:** If you're US-based but have EU donors, their data crosses borders.

### Current Situation (Post-Schrems II)

**The Problem:**
- EU-US Privacy Shield was invalidated in 2020
- Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) are still valid but need additional safeguards
- US surveillance laws create compliance challenges

**For Most Non-Profits:**
- Low risk if processing is limited and appropriate safeguards exist
- Focus on Standard Contractual Clauses + additional security measures
- Document your risk assessment

### Transfer Safeguards Checklist

**Minimum Requirements:**

✅ **Use Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs):**
- Most major vendors provide these (Google, Microsoft, Salesforce)
- New 2021 SCCs include transfer impact assessment
- Sign and retain copies

✅ **Implement Technical Safeguards:**
- Encryption at rest and in transit
- Access controls and authentication
- Data minimization (only transfer what's necessary)
- Regular security testing

✅ **Conduct Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA):**
```
TRANSFER IMPACT ASSESSMENT
Vendor: [Name]
Data Types: [List]
Data Subjects: [EU donors, volunteers, etc.]

Risk Assessment:
- Data sensitivity: [Low/Medium/High]
- Volume: [Number of records]
- Nature of processing: [Description]

Safeguards in Place:
- SCCs signed: [Date]
- Encryption: [Type]
- Access controls: [Description]
- Data retention: [Period]

Conclusion: [Acceptable risk with safeguards / Needs additional measures]
Date: [Date]
Review Date: [Annual]
```

✅ **Consider Data Localization:**
- Some vendors offer EU data centers
- Ask vendors about data location options
- May reduce compliance burden
