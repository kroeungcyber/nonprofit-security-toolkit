# 🏢 Physical Security Guide

**Protect your office, equipment, and paper records from theft, unauthorized access, and disasters.**

---

## Why This Matters

Digital security is important, but physical security is just as critical.

**Real incidents:**
- A non-profit's office was burglarized; thieves stole laptops with unencrypted donor data
- Volunteer left sensitive files on a desk overnight; cleaning crew had access
- Former employee walked out with a thumb drive containing all volunteer records
- Fire destroyed office; no off-site backups meant years of records lost

**Physical security protects:**
- Computers and mobile devices
- Paper files and donor records
- Network equipment and servers
- Office supplies and assets
- Staff and volunteers

---

## What You'll Accomplish

- ✅ Secure your office space
- ✅ Control visitor and volunteer access
- ✅ Protect equipment from theft
- ✅ Ensure secure disposal of sensitive materials
- ✅ Prepare for emergencies and disasters

**Time to implement:** 3-4 hours initially, then ongoing maintenance  
**Cost:** $0-200 (basic measures) to $500+ (comprehensive)  
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Step 1: Office Access Control

### Key and Access Control

**The Problem:**
Too many people have keys. Keys get lost. You never know who has access.

**The Solution:**
Implement a controlled key/access system.

#### Key Management System

```
KEY/ACCESS CONTROL LOG

Location/Area: ___________________________

Key Holder          Type of Access    Date Issued    Date Returned    Notes
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[Name]              Front door key    [Date]         [Date]           
[Name]              Office key        [Date]         [Date]           
[Name]              File cabinet key  [Date]         [Date]           

MASTER KEY HOLDERS
Name: _________________ Keys Held: _________________ Emergency Contact: _______
Name: _________________ Keys Held: _________________ Emergency Contact: _______

KEY INVENTORY
Total Keys Made: _______
Keys Accounted For: _______
Missing Keys: _______
Last Audit Date: _______
```

#### Key Control Best Practices

**✅ DO:**
- Number all keys
- Keep a log of who has which keys
- Require signatures when keys are issued/returned
- Collect keys immediately when staff/volunteers leave
- Rekey locks when keys are lost
- Limit master keys to 2-3 people maximum
- Store spare keys in a secure lockbox (not labeled!)

**❌ DON'T:**
- Let people make unauthorized copies
- Leave keys unattended on desks
- Label keys with what they open
- Give keys to volunteers without approval
- Forget to collect keys on departure

#### Modern Alternatives

**Electronic Keypads (Recommended):**
- No physical keys to lose
- Change codes easily
- Create unique codes for each person
- Track who enters when
- Cost: $100-300

**Smart Locks:**
- Phone/app-based access
- Temporary access for volunteers
- Remote access for emergencies
- Activity logging
- Cost: $200-400

**Key Lockbox (Budget Option):**
- Secure mounted box with combination
- Store spare keys inside
- Share combination only with authorized people
- Cost: $30-60

### Visitor Management

**The Problem:**
You don't know who's in your office or why.

**The Solution:**
Simple visitor tracking.

#### Visitor Log

```
VISITOR LOG

Date: ___________

Time In | Time Out | Name | Organization | Purpose | Contact | Escort
────────┼──────────┼──────┼──────────────┼─────────┼─────────┼────────
        │          │      │              │         │         │
        │          │      │              │         │         │
        │          │      │              │         │         │

VISITOR BADGES ISSUED: _______
BADGES RETURNED: _______
```

#### Visitor Procedures

**For All Visitors:**

1. **Sign In**
   - Name and organization
   - Who they're visiting
   - Purpose of visit
   - Contact information

2. **Badge/Identification**
   - Issue temporary visitor badge
   - Must be worn visibly
   - Collect when they leave

3. **Escort Policy**
   - Visitors must be escorted in restricted areas
   - Volunteers can escort visitors they know
   - Don't leave visitors unattended

4. **Sign Out**
   - Record departure time
   - Collect badge
   - Thank them for visiting

**Restricted Areas (Define These):**
- Server/network equipment room
- File storage areas
- Executive offices (if sensitive)
- Areas with sensitive data visible

## Step 2: Equipment Security

### Laptop and Device Security

**The Problem:**
Laptops are stolen, left unattended, or borrowed without permission.

**Solutions:**

#### Physical Protection

**Cable Locks:**
- Kensington-style cable locks
- Loop through desk or heavy furniture
- Cost: $20-40 per device
- Prevents grab-and-go theft

**Secure Storage:**
- Lockable cabinets for laptops when not in use
- Secure laptop bags for transport
- Store in locked office overnight

**Laptop Checkout System:**

```
LAPTOP/EQUIPMENT CHECKOUT LOG

Device: _________________ Serial #: _________________

Date Out | Date Due | Borrower | Purpose | Condition Out | Condition In | Staff Initial
─────────┼──────────┼──────────┼─────────┼───────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────
         │          │          │         │               │              │
         │          │          │         │               │              │

CONDITION CODES:
E = Excellent  G = Good  F = Fair  D = Damaged  M = Missing

CURRENT STATUS: ☐ Available ☐ Checked Out ☐ Maintenance ☐ Retired
```

#### Device Security Checklist

**Daily:**
- ☐ Lock laptops when unattended (Windows + L)
- ☐ Store in locked drawer/cabinet overnight
- ☐ Never leave visible in cars
- ☐ Use cable locks in public spaces

**When Traveling:**
- ☐ Keep with you at all times
- ☐ Don't check laptops in luggage
- ☐ Use privacy screens in public
- ☐ Backup before traveling

**For Loaner Devices:**
- ☐ Full disk encryption required
- ☐ No sensitive data stored locally
- ☐ VPN required for network access
- ☐ Return checklist completed

### Paper File Security

**The Problem:**
Sensitive files are left on desks, in unlocked cabinets, or improperly disposed of.

**Solutions:**

#### File Storage

**Lockable Filing Cabinets:**
- All sensitive files in locked cabinets
- Separate cabinets by sensitivity level
- Limit keys to authorized personnel
- Label cabinets by contents (general terms only)

**Clean Desk Policy:**

```
CLEAN DESK POLICY

All staff and volunteers must:

☐ Lock sensitive documents in drawers when leaving desk
☐ Lock computer screen (Windows + L) when away
☐ Secure laptops with cable locks or in locked cabinets
☐ Clear desk of confidential materials at end of day
☐ Shred unneeded sensitive documents immediately
☐ Post-it notes with passwords are PROHIBITED

WHEN YOU LEAVE YOUR DESK (even for 5 minutes):
- Lock your computer
- Put sensitive papers in drawer
- Take mobile devices with you

END OF DAY:
- Clear desk completely
- Lock all cabinets
- Store laptops in secure location
- Turn off or lock office lights

APPLIES TO:
☐ Staff
☐ Volunteers
☐ Interns
☐ Visitors (must be escorted)
```

#### Sensitive Document Handling

**What is "Sensitive":**
- Donor personal information
- Financial records
- Volunteer background checks
- Beneficiary/client data
- Board meeting minutes
- Strategic plans
- Contracts and legal documents

**Handling Procedures:**

**When Printing:**
- Collect immediately from printer
- Don't leave sitting in output tray
- Securely store or shred

**When Mailing:**
- Use opaque envelopes
- Mark "Confidential" if appropriate
- Use tracking for sensitive items
- Verify recipient address

**When Faxing:**
- Use cover sheets
- Confirm recipient is available
- Don't leave original at machine
- Shred if sensitive

**When Scanning:**
- Delete digital copies when done (if not needed)
- Secure the original
- Don't leave on scanner bed

## Step 3: Secure Disposal

### Document Destruction

**The Problem:**
Sensitive papers are thrown in regular trash or recycling.

**The Solution:**
Proper shredding procedures.

#### What to Shred

**ALWAYS Shred:**
- ☐ Anything with donor names and addresses
- ☐ Financial documents older than retention period
- ☐ Background check results
- ☐ Staff personal information
- ☐ Passwords or access codes (even if old)
- ☐ Draft documents with sensitive data
- ☐ Meeting notes with confidential discussions
- ☐ Travel itineraries (reveals when office is empty)

**CAN Throw Away:**
- General correspondence (non-sensitive)
- Marketing materials
- Public reports
- Newspapers and magazines
- Packaging materials

#### Shredding Options

**Cross-Cut Shredder (Recommended):**
- Cuts paper into small pieces (not strips)
- Harder to reassemble
- Cost: $50-150
- Capacity: 10-20 sheets at a time

**Shredding Service:**
- For large volumes
- Secure pickup or on-site shredding
- Certificate of destruction provided
- Cost: $50-200 depending on volume

**Burning (If Permitted):**
- Most secure method
- Check local fire regulations
- Not practical for most offices

#### Shred Bin System

```
SHRED BIN LOCATIONS

Office Location          Bin Type        Emptied By      Schedule
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Reception                Locked console  Office manager  Weekly
Staff area               Open bin        All staff       Daily
File room                Locked console  Admin           Monthly
Volunteer area           Open bin        Volunteers      Daily

LOCKED CONSOLE KEYS HELD BY:
- Office Manager: _______
- Backup: _______

SHREDDING SERVICE:
Company: _______
Contact: _______
Schedule: _______
Certificate of Destruction kept: _______
```

#### Digital Media Disposal

**What Requires Secure Disposal:**
- Hard drives (computers, external drives)
- USB drives and thumb drives
- CDs/DVDs with data
- Old backup tapes
- Mobile devices (phones, tablets)
- Printer/scanner hard drives

**Disposal Methods:**

**Hard Drives:**
1. **Software Wipe** (For reuse/sale):
   - Use DBAN (Darik's Boot and Nuke) - free
   - Multiple overwrite passes
   - Verify wipe completion

2. **Physical Destruction** (Most secure):
   - Remove drive from computer
   - Drill holes through platters
   - Or use professional destruction service

**USB/CDs/DVDs:**
- Cut/shred CDs and DVDs
- Physical destruction of USB drives (crush or cut)
- Or use hammer/nail to destroy chip

**Mobile Devices:**
- Factory reset
- Remove SIM and SD cards
- Destroy separately
- Consider professional recycling

**Printer Hard Drives:**
- Many printers store copies of printed documents
- Check manual for wipe procedures
- Or remove and destroy hard drive

#### Disposal Documentation

```
SECURE DISPOSAL LOG

Date       Item Type         Serial/ID      Method       Witness    Notes
────────── ┌────────────────┼──────────────┼────────────┼──────────┼─────────
           │                │              │            │          │
           │                │              │            │          │
           │                │              │            │          │

DESTRUCTION METHODS:
S = Software wipe (DBAN)
P = Physical destruction
R = Professional recycling service
C = Certificate of destruction obtained

WITNESS REQUIREMENT:
All hard drive destruction must be witnessed by 2 people.
Both sign this log.
```

## Step 4: Office Security Measures

### Entry Points

**Doors:**
- Solid core or metal doors (not hollow)
- Deadbolts on exterior doors
- Door closers (auto-close)
- Peepholes or window to see visitors
- Strike plates secured with 3" screws

**Windows:**
- Lockable windows
- Security film (prevents shattering)
- Window locks or pins
- Consider bars for ground-floor windows in high-crime areas
- Don't leave windows open when office is empty

**Lighting:**
- Motion-activated exterior lights
- Interior lights on timers (when away)
- Well-lit parking areas
- Remove hiding spots near entry

**Alarms (If Budget Allows):**
- Door/window sensors
- Motion detectors
- Glass break sensors
- 24/7 monitoring service
- Fire/smoke detection
- Cost: $30-100/month

---

### Equipment Room Security

**If You Have a Server/Network Room:**

**Physical Controls:**
- Lockable door (separate from general office)
- Limited access (2-3 people max)
- Visitor escort required

- Fire suppression system
- Climate control (temperature/humidity)
- UPS (battery backup)
- Equipment anchored to prevent theft

## Step 6: Special Situations

### Shared Office Spaces

**If You Share Space With Other Organizations:**

**Security Considerations:**
- Separate network (don't share WiFi)
- Lockable storage for your equipment
- Separate file storage
- Visitor procedures coordinated
- After-hours access controlled
- Shared alarm codes? Change regularly

**Agreements to Put in Place:**
- Shared security responsibilities
- Visitor policies
- After-hours access rules
- Incident notification procedures

### Remote Work Security

**For Staff Working From Home:**

**Physical Security Requirements:**
- Lockable door/office space
- Screen privacy (can't be viewed by family/visitors)
- Secure storage for work devices
- Shredder for sensitive documents
- No work in public spaces where screens visible

**Procedures:**
- Return all equipment when employment ends
- No printing of sensitive documents at home (if possible)
- Secure disposal of any printed materials
- Report lost/stolen equipment immediately
- No unauthorized people using work devices

### Event Security

**For Fundraising Events:**

**Before Event:**
- Remove/secure sensitive files from event space
- Lock offices not in use
- Brief staff on security procedures
- Have plan for cash handling

**During Event:**
- Monitor entry points
- Restrict access to office areas
- Secure valuables
- Document any security incidents

**After Event:**
- Secure all cash/donations
- Lock all doors
- Check all areas before leaving
