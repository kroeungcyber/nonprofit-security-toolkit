# 🎓 Volunteer Training Guide

**Teach your team to recognize and report phishing emails.**

---

## Why This Matters

Even with the best technical protections, **one click by an untrained volunteer can compromise your entire organization.**

The good news: Phishing emails follow patterns. Once you know what to look for, they're easy to spot.

**Training goal:** Make your volunteers your first line of defense, not your weakest link.

---

## Training Format Options

Choose what works for your team:

### Option 1: Group Training Session (30-45 minutes)
- Best for: Organizations with regular volunteer meetings
- Format: Presentation + Q&A + quiz

### Option 2: Self-Paced Learning (20 minutes)
- Best for: Remote volunteers or busy schedules
- Format: Share this guide + video links

### Option 3: Email Series (5 minutes per day for 5 days)
- Best for: Volunteers who prefer bite-sized learning
- Format: Daily email with one concept + example

## The Training Content

### Part 1: What is Phishing? (5 minutes)

**Simple definition:**
> Phishing is when someone sends you a fake email pretending to be someone you trust, trying to trick you into giving them information, money, or access to your computer.

**Real examples from non-profits:**
- Fake donor promising $50,000 if you click a link
- Fake "Google security alert" asking you to verify your password
- Fake email from the "IRS" threatening to revoke tax-exempt status
- Fake invoice from a vendor you actually use

**Key message:**
> "If something feels off, it probably is. When in doubt, check it out."

---

### Part 2: The 5 Red Flags (10 minutes)

Teach volunteers to look for these warning signs:

#### 🚩 Red Flag #1: Urgent or Threatening Language

**What to watch for:**
- "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!"
- "URGENT: Action required immediately!"
- "Final notice before legal action!"
- "You've been hacked! Click here to secure your account!"

**Why it's a red flag:** Scammers want you to act before you think.

**What to do:** Take a breath. Real organizations don't demand immediate action via email.

---

#### 🚩 Red Flag #2: Generic Greetings

**What to watch for:**
- "Dear Customer"
- "Dear User"
- "Dear Valued Member"
- No greeting at all

**Why it's a red flag:** Legitimate organizations usually know your name. Scammers send millions of emails and don't personalize them.

**Note:** Some real emails use generic greetings too. Look for multiple red flags.

---

#### 🚩 Red Flag #3: Suspicious Links

**How to check:**
1. Hover your mouse over the link (DON'T CLICK)
2. Look at the bottom of your browser window
3. Check if the URL matches what it claims to be

**Example:**
- Link says: "Verify your PayPal account"
- Actual URL: `http://paypa1-security.verify-account.com`
- **This is fake!** (Notice the "1" instead of "l" in "paypal")

**What to do:** If the URL looks wrong, don't click. Go directly to the website by typing it in your browser.

---

#### 🚩 Red Flag #4: Requests for Sensitive Information

**What to watch for:**
- "Please confirm your password"
- "Enter your social security number"
- "Update your credit card information"
- "Verify your bank account details"

**Why it's a red flag:** Legitimate organizations will NEVER ask for passwords or sensitive info via email.

**What to do:** Delete the email. If you're concerned, contact the organization directly using a phone number from their official website (not from the email).

---

#### 🚩 Red Flag #5: Unexpected Attachments

**What to watch for:**
- You weren't expecting a file
- The file type is unusual (.exe, .zip, .js, .scr)
- The sender asks you to "enable macros" or "enable editing"

**Why it's a red flag:** Attachments can contain viruses.

**What to do:** Don't open unexpected attachments. Contact the sender through a different method to confirm they sent it.

### Part 3: Common Non-Profit Scams (10 minutes)

#### Scam #1: The Fake Major Donor

**What it looks like:**
```
Subject: Urgent Donation Opportunity

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing to inform you of my intention to donate $75,000 to your 
organization. I have been following your work and am deeply impressed.

To process this donation, please click the link below and provide your 
banking details for the wire transfer.

[Click Here to Receive Donation]

God bless,
Robert Johnson
```

**Why it's fake:**
- Generic greeting ("Dear Sir/Madam")
- Unrealistic donation amount out of the blue
- Asks for banking details via email
- Pressure to click a link

**Real donors don't:** Email out of the blue with huge offers and ask for banking information.

---

#### Scam #2: The Fake Executive Request

**What it looks like:**
```
Subject: URGENT - Need Your Help

Hi [Your Name],

I'm in back-to-back meetings and can't take calls. I need you to do 
something urgently.

Can you purchase $500 in Amazon gift cards for a client appreciation 
event? Scratch off the codes and reply with the numbers. I'll reimburse 
you immediately.

This is time-sensitive. Please confirm you received this.

Thanks,
[Executive's Name]
Sent from my iPhone
```

**Why it's fake:**
- Unusual request (gift cards)
- "I'm in meetings" prevents you from verifying
- Urgent pressure
- Slightly different email address (check carefully!)

**What to do:** Call or text the person directly to verify. Never buy gift cards based on email alone.

---

#### Scam #3: The Fake Vendor Invoice

**What it looks like:**
```
Subject: Past Due Invoice #48392

ATTENTION REQUIRED

Our records indicate your payment of $2,400 is 30 days overdue. 
Please see attached invoice.

To avoid late fees and service interruption, please remit payment 
immediately using the updated banking information attached.

Regards,
ABC Office Supplies
```

**Why it's fake:**
- You don't recognize the invoice number
- "Updated banking information" is a red flag
- Urgent threat (service interruption)
- Attachment you weren't expecting

**What to do:** Don't open the attachment. Contact your regular vendor contact using a known phone number to verify.

---

### Part 4: What To Do When You Spot Phishing (5 minutes)

#### If You Haven't Clicked Anything:

✅ **DO:**
- Report it to your supervisor or IT contact
- Delete the email
- Warn others if you think they might have received it too

❌ **DON'T:**
- Reply to the email
- Click any links
- Open any attachments
- Forward it to anyone (except your IT person)

---

#### If You Clicked a Link or Entered Your Password:

🚨 **ACT IMMEDIATELY:**

1. **Change your password RIGHT NOW**
   - Log into the real website directly (don't use the email link)
   - Change your password immediately
   - Use a strong, unique password

2. **Enable 2FA if you haven't already**
   - See the [Two-Factor Authentication guide](../01-protect-your-people/two-factor-authentication.md)

3. **Tell your supervisor or IT contact immediately**
   - They need to know in case others got the same email
   - They can check for suspicious activity

4. **Check your account for unauthorized activity**
   - Look for emails you didn't send
   - Check login history if available
   - Look for changed settings

5. **Scan your computer for malware**
   - Run a full antivirus scan
   - Look for unusual programs or browser extensions

---

#### If You Opened an Attachment:

1. **Disconnect from the internet temporarily**
   - This prevents malware from communicating with attackers

2. **Run a full antivirus scan**

3. **Contact your IT person immediately**

4. **Don't enter any passwords** until you're sure the computer is clean

### Part 5: Test Your Knowledge (5 minutes)

**Quiz: Is this phishing or legitimate?**

**Question 1:**
```
From: security@amaz0n.com
Subject: Your account has been locked

Dear Customer,

We have detected unusual activity on your account. Click here immediately 
to verify your identity or your account will be permanently deleted.

[Verify Now]
```

**Answer:** 🚨 **PHISHING**
- Email domain is "amaz0n" with a zero, not "amazon"
- Generic greeting
- Urgent threat
- Asks to click a link

---

**Question 2:**
```
From: sarah@localcommunityfoundation.org
Subject: Following up on our meeting

Hi John,

Thanks again for meeting with me yesterday. As promised, I'm sending 
the grant application guidelines we discussed.

Let me know if you have any questions.

Best,
Sarah Chen
Program Officer
```

**Answer:** ✅ **LIKELY LEGITIMATE**
- Specific reference to a meeting
- Natural, professional tone
- No urgent requests
- No suspicious links
- (But still verify if you weren't expecting it!)

---

**Question 3:**
```
From: director@yourorg.org
Subject: Quick favor?

Are you available? I need you to process a wire transfer immediately 
for an important donor. The bank details are attached. This can't wait.

I'm heading into a meeting and will be unreachable for 2 hours.

Thanks!
```

**Answer:** 🚨 **SUSPECT — VERIFY FIRST**
- Unusual request (wire transfer)
- Urgent pressure
- "I'll be unreachable" prevents verification
- Check: Is this actually from your director's real email address?

**What to do:** Call or text your director directly to verify.

## Training Follow-Up

### After the Training

**Send a summary email:**
```
Subject: Phishing Training - Key Takeaways

Hi team,

Thanks for attending today's security training. Here are the key points:

1. Phishing emails try to trick you into giving away passwords or money
2. Watch for the 5 red flags: urgency, generic greetings, suspicious 
   links, requests for info, unexpected attachments
3. When in doubt, check it out — ask before clicking
4. If you click something suspicious, change your password immediately 
   and tell [contact person]

Attached: Phishing protection reference card (print and keep handy!)

Questions? Reply to this email or contact [IT person].

Thanks for helping keep our organization secure!
```

### Quarterly Refreshers

**Send periodic reminders:**
- "Phishing Friday" — Share one example per month
- "Security Spotlight" — Highlight a recent real-world attack
- "Quiz Time" — Send a sample email and ask if it's phishing

---

## Advanced: Simulated Phishing Tests

Once your team is trained, consider running practice tests:

1. **Use free tools:**
   - [Gophish](https://getgophish.com/) (open source)
   - [KnowBe4 Free Tools](https://www.knowbe4.com/free-phishing-security-test)

2. **Send a fake phishing email** to your team

3. **See who clicks** (they get directed to a training page)

4. **Follow up with additional training** for those who clicked

**Important:** Always announce that you're doing this beforehand. You want to train, not trick.
